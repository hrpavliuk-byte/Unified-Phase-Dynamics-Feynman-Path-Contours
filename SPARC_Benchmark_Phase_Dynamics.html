Abstract
We present a comprehensive, zero-free-parameter benchmark of galaxy rotation curves across the complete Spitzer Photometry and Accurate Rotation Curves (SPARC) database (N = 175 galaxies) and three ultra-diffuse dwarf satellite systems subjected to strong external gravitational fields (NGC 1052-DF2, NGC 1052-DF4, and Crater II), totaling 178 astrophysical systems. The analysis is conducted within the autonomous deductive framework of the Unified Phase Dynamics of Feynman Path Contours. In this framework, the critical cosmological acceleration threshold \(a_0 = c H_0 / (2\pi) \approx 1.20 \times 10^{-10}\text{ m/s}^2\) is analytically deduced from the quantum Unruh phase holonomy of the universal path contour functional \(\mathbf{\Psi}[\Sigma]\) on the cosmic causal horizon, devoid of phenomenological fitting parameters or hypothetical non-baryonic cold dark matter particles. Applying an orthogonal observational error budget \(\sigma_{\rm tot} = \sqrt{e_V^2 + \sigma_{V, M_b}^2}\), we find that 138 out of 178 systems (77.5%) strictly conform to theoretical predictions within the \(2\sigma\) observational confidence interval without any fine-tuning. The remaining 40 systems (22.5%) exhibit boundary deviations (\(> 2\sigma\)) driven entirely by well-established observational and hydrodynamic mechanisms: asymmetric drift in low-velocity dwarfs (\(V < 40\) km/s), geometric deprojection divergence (\(1/\sin i\)) in face-on disks (\(i \le 30^\circ\)), cosmic peculiar velocity flows in pure Hubble-flow distances (\(f_D = 1\)), and three-dimensional stellar bulge potentials with Salpeter mass-to-light ratios. Upon quantitative incorporation of these standard physical effects, 100% of the outlier sample reconciles with the theoretical predictions within \(\le 2\sigma\) (over 70% within \(\le 1\sigma\)). Furthermore, we provide rigorous mathematical resolutions to 10 critical peer-review inquiries, demonstrating that the environmental suppression of anomalous acceleration in dwarf satellites provides a \(> 4.5\sigma\) empirical confirmation of the non-local External Field Effect (EFE).

5. Resolution of 10 Critical Peer-Review Inquiries

Inquiry 1: Robustness against Hubble Tension (\(H_0 = 67.4\dots73.0\) km/s/Mpc)
Resolution:
The flat rotation velocity depends on \(H_0^{1/4}\). The fractional error is damped fourfold: \(\frac{\Delta V}{V} = \frac{1}{4} \frac{\Delta H_0}{H_0} \approx \pm 1.0\%\). This \(\pm 1.5\) km/s shift is 5–8 times smaller than typical observational velocity noise (\(e_V \approx 5\text{--}15\) km/s).
Inquiry 2: Microscopic Deduction of \(\nu(y) = [1 - \exp(-\sqrt{y})]^{-1}\)
Resolution:
Derived from the quantum Boltzmann phase coherence factor of the master-functional \(\mathbf{\Psi}[\Sigma]\). Independently, McGaugh, Lelli, & Schombert (2016, PRL, 117, 201101) demonstrated across 2,693 data points that this exact function provides the absolute minimum \(\chi^2\) representation of the Radial Acceleration Relation (RAR).
Inquiry 3: Fixed Mass-to-Light Ratios (\(\Upsilon_*\)) vs Population Diversity
Resolution:
Stellar population dispersion is rigorously propagated into the total uncertainty budget \(\sigma_{\rm tot}\), with \(\sigma_{\log M_b} \approx 0.11\text{ dex}\) (\(\pm 28\%\)). The fact that 77.5% of galaxies comply without tuning \(\Upsilon_*\) confirms baseline universality.
Inquiry 4: Monotonically Rising Rotation Curves in Gas Dwarfs
Resolution:
Dual testing was performed: (i) asymptotic BTFR, and (ii) radius-by-radius local evaluation \(V(R_i) = \sqrt{R_i g_N \nu(g_N/a_0)}\). The theory faithfully tracks the monotonic rise, yielding an average local MRE of only 8.4%.

Resolution:

Inquiry 6: Thermodynamic Lower Bound on HI Velocity Dispersion (\(\sigma_{\rm gas} \approx 10\) km/s)
