Consequently, lepton number is strictly conserved ($\Delta L \equiv 0$), the Weinberg dimension-5 operator vanishes identically ($\mathcal{O}_5 \equiv 0$), and neutrinos are proven to be strictly Dirac fermions. This provides an analytical resolution to the 40-year null experimental results in neutrinoless double beta decay ($0\nu\beta\beta$), predicting exact null observables ($m_{\beta\beta} \equiv 0, T_{1/2}^{0\nu\beta\beta} \to \infty$) for KamLAND-Zen, GERDA, LEGEND-1000, and nEXO. 1. Introduction and Theoretical Dilemma

The origin of neutrino mass and the fundamental nature of lepton flavor mixing remain among the deepest unresolved puzzles in theoretical physics. In the minimal Standard Model (SM), neutrinos are strictly massless because right-handed neutrino fields $\nu_R$ are absent, and $SU(2)_L \times U(1)_Y$ gauge invariance forbids a bare mass term.

To explain the compelling empirical evidence for neutrino oscillations observed in solar, atmospheric, reactor, and accelerator experiments, mainstream physics routinely introduces one of two phenomenological hypotheses:

  1. Type-I Seesaw Mechanism: Introducing three ultra-heavy, gauge-singlet Majorana neutrinos with masses near the grand unification scale ($M_R \sim 10^{14}\text{--}10^{15}\text{ GeV}$). The light active neutrino masses are generated via the effective Weinberg dimension-5 operator: $$\mathcal{O}_5 = \frac{c_5}{\Lambda} (L H)^2 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad m_\nu \sim \frac{v^2}{M_R}.$$ However, the Majorana hypothesis suffers from severe conceptual and empirical vulnerabilities: over forty years of exhaustive underground searches for neutrinoless double beta decay ($0\nu\beta\beta$) in $^{76}\mathrm{Ge}$, $^{136}\mathrm{Xe}$, and $^{130}\mathrm{Te}$ (KamLAND-Zen, GERDA, CUORE, EXO-200, Majorana Demonstrator) have yielded persistent null results, pushing half-life limits past $T_{1/2}^{0\nu\beta\beta} > 2.3 \times 10^{26}\text{ yr}$ and effective mass limits to $m_{\beta\beta} < 36\text{--}156\text{ meV}$.
  2. Dirac Yukawa Fine-Tuning: Introducing right-handed neutrinos $\nu_R$ with purely Dirac masses, while fine-tuning the dimensionless Yukawa couplings to $y_\nu \sim 10^{-12}$, which lacks any dynamical or symmetry-based explanation within local effective field theory.

Furthermore, cosmological measurements have recently placed stringent upper limits on the sum of neutrino masses. Combining cosmic microwave background (CMB) measurements from Planck with baryon acoustic oscillations (BAO) from the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI 2024 Year-1 results) yields the restrictive ceiling: $$\sum m_\nu < 0.072\text{ eV} \quad (95\%\text{ CL}).$$ This bound severely challenges inverted mass ordering ($m_3 \ll m_1 < m_2$, which requires $\sum m_\nu \ge 0.10\text{ eV}$) and strongly constrains quasi-degenerate mass spectra, favoring minimal normal ordering ($m_1 < m_2 < m_3$) with a vanishingly light lightest state.
