\section{Introduction and Theoretical Dilemma}

The origin of neutrino mass and the fundamental nature of lepton flavor mixing remain among the deepest unresolved puzzles in theoretical physics \cite{Pontecorvo1957, Maki1962}. \begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Type-I Seesaw Mechanism:} Introducing three ultra-heavy, gauge-singlet Majorana neutrinos with masses near the grand unification scale ($M_R \sim 10^{14}\text{--}10^{15}\text{ GeV}$) \cite{Minkowski1977, GellMann1979, Yanagida1979, Mohapatra1980}.     \begin{equation}
        \mathcal{O}_5 = \frac{c_5}{\Lambda} (L H)^2 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad m_\nu \sim \frac{v^2}{M_R}.
        \label{eq:weinberg_op}
    \end{equation}
    However, the Majorana hypothesis suffers from severe conceptual and empirical vulnerabilities:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item The heavy states $N_R$ are fundamentally unobservable in laboratory colliders, introducing an ad-hoc, untestable energy scale.
        \item Over forty years of exhaustive underground searches for neutrinoless double beta decay ($0\nu\beta\beta$) in $^{76}\mathrm{Ge}$, $^{136}\mathrm{Xe}$, and $^{130}\mathrm{Te}$ (KamLAND-Zen, GERDA, CUORE, EXO-200, Majorana Demonstrator) have yielded persistent null results \cite{KamLANDZen2023, GERDA2020, CUORE2021}, pushing the half-life bound past $T_{1/2}^{0\nu\beta\beta} > 2.3 \times 10^{26}\text{ yr}$ and effective mass limits to $m_{\beta\beta} < 36\text{--}156\text{ meV}$.

    \item \textbf{Dirac Yukawa Fine-Tuning:} Introducing right-handed neutrinos $\nu_R$ with purely Dirac masses, while fine-tuning the dimensionless Yukawa couplings to $y_\nu \sim 10^{-12}$, which lacks any dynamical or symmetry-based explanation within local effective field theory.

Furthermore, cosmological measurements have recently placed stringent upper limits on the sum of neutrino masses. Combining cosmic microwave background (CMB) measurements from the Planck satellite with baryon acoustic oscillations (BAO) from the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI 2024 Year-1 results) yields the restrictive ceiling \cite{DESI2024}:
\begin{equation}

\end{equation}
This bound severely challenges inverted mass ordering ($m_3 \ll m_1 < m_2$, which requires $\sum m_\nu \ge 0.10\text{ eV}$) and strongly constrains quasi-degenerate mass spectra, favoring minimal normal ordering ($m_1 < m_2 < m_3$) with a vanishingly light lightest state.
